We curate publications in sustainability science that address what we take to be the field's core challenge: understanding and navigating nature-society systems (complex adaptive systems, coupled human-environment dynamics, socio-ecological systems) to enable transformations toward more equitable development pathways for both current and future generations.

Updated monthly.

---

## July 2026

**1. Actions and actors driving transformative change for global sustainability**

Reyes-García, V., Krug, R.M., Agrawal, A., et al. · *Nature Sustainability* · Feb 2026

Large-scale bibliometric analysis identifying which actors and mechanisms actually drive transformative change. Moves beyond theory to show what works in practice.

[Read paper →](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41893-026-01783-1)

---

**2. Advancing biodiversity through poverty solutions**

Pradhan, N., Ibáñez, I., Chhatre, A., et al. · *Nature Sustainability* · Mar 2026

Reveals that poverty reduction and biodiversity conservation aren't trade-offs. Poor households can be better forest stewards than wealthier ones.

[Read paper →](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41893-026-01816-9)

---

**3. Realising environmental justice: Strategy and tactics for transformation**

Schlosberg, D., Rickards, L., Della Bosca, H., et al. · *EPC: Politics and Space* · Feb 2026

Maps three distinct approaches to environmental justice organizing. There's no single playbook, but multiple strategies that environmental justice scholars identify as effective.

[Read paper →](https://doi.org/10.1177/23996544261430347)

---

**4. Social movements are transformative agents for biodiversity conservation**

Walter, M., Scheidel, A., Vadez, C., et al. · *Sustainability Science* · Dec 2025

Documents 2,801 environmental justice mobilizations and shows ~40% occur within biodiversity hotspots. Civil society, not just conservation professionals, is driving biodiversity protection.

[Read paper →](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2520218122)

---

**5. Smallholder farmers' adaptation at the climate–conflict nexus: a systematic review**

Rubangakene, D., Oryema, C., Nielsen, M.R. · *Frontiers in Climate* · Feb 2026

First systematic synthesis of how smallholder farmers adapt when climate stress and violent conflict happen together. Adaptation strategies look different when both threats are present.

[Read paper →](https://doi.org/10.3389/fclim.2026.1699078)
