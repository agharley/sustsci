# The Alaskan Salmon Fishery: Managing Resources in a Globalizing World

![Salmon fishing sailboat at Clark's Point, Bristol Bay, Alaska]({{ '/assets/images/cases/alaska.jpg' | relative_url }})
<p style="font-size:0.85em; font-style:italic; color:#2874a6; margin-top:8px;">
Salmon fishing sailboat at Clark's Point, Bristol Bay. Photo: Alaska State Library, John E. Thwaites Photograph Collection, P18-118.
</p>

**[Michaela Thompson](https://www.michaelathompson.net/)**

The Alaskan salmon fishery is often held up as a model of successful, sustainable resource management — one of the few major fisheries to have avoided catastrophic collapse. This case looks at the harder story beneath that reputation. Alaska has long been imagined as "The Last Frontier," a land of inexhaustible resources and untamed wilderness; the case traces how that perception collided with the realities of managing a living resource embedded in global markets — how a fishery that began as a source of local livelihoods became globally embedded, and what it has taken, and still takes, to manage extraction under the pressures of globalization and consumption demand.

The case pays particular attention to Bristol Bay and its famed sockeye fishery, using the region to examine knowledge production, environmental risk, and collaboration — and, through them, the impact of thickening connections and globalization on regional development.

## Using this case in the course

The Alaska case is the first case students meet: it is assigned in [Unit 1.1](/course/part-1-conceptual-challenge/unit-1-1-framework.html) as the opening test of the course's framework for sustainability analysis, and returns in [Unit 1.5](/course/part-1-conceptual-challenge/unit-1-5-integrated-assessment.html) (integrated assessment), [Unit 2.4](/course/part-2-anthropocene/unit-2-4-horizontal-connections.html) (horizontal connections), [Unit 2.5](/course/part-2-anthropocene/unit-2-5-vertical-connections.html) (vertical connections), and [Unit 2.8](/course/part-2-anthropocene/unit-2-8-synthesis.html) (synthesis). Used this way, the case lets students watch the ideas of successive units accumulate around a single place — and practice the essential work of fitting general ideas to context. Sustainable development is a long, intergenerational process; cases that span generations let students watch development pathways — and efforts to bend them — unfold at the time scales that matter.
