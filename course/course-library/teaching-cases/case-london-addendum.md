# Looking Outward: Refocusing Attention on London's Hinterland

![West India Docks, London, 1810 — ships from the colonial trade]({{ '/assets/images/cases/london-addendum.jpg' | relative_url }})

**Alicia G. Harley**

This short companion to the [London case](case-london.html) was written after we realized that the case's telling of London's story had — perhaps a little too ironically — largely ignored a critical spatial dimension of the city's development: the distant places and resources on which London relied as it grew. A city's development pathway is never only local. The timber, sugar, grain, and labor that sustained London's rise came from hinterlands that stretched, by the height of empire, across much of the globe — and the consequences of London's growth landed in those places too. The addendum refocuses attention outward, using London's history to explore the horizontal connections among places ([Unit 2.4](/course/part-2-anthropocene/unit-2-4-horizontal-connections.html)) that any serious account of urban sustainability has to include.

*A revised and expanded version of this addendum is in preparation and will appear as a working paper; this page will be updated with the formal citation when it is published.*

## Using this case in the course

The addendum is assigned in [Unit 2.4](/course/part-2-anthropocene/unit-2-4-horizontal-connections.html) (horizontal connections), after students have worked with the main London case — the point in the course where the question "sustainable for whom, and at whose expense?" acquires its geographic edge.
